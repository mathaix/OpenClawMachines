The Communication Problem

Property management isn't hard because of the properties. It's hard because every unit comes with a human who needs things — and they all need them at the same time. A tenant with a broken AC at 11 PM. A prospect who wants to see the unit tomorrow morning. An owner who wants to know why last month's rent was late. Every message is urgent to the person sending it. And every hour you don't respond, you lose something: a tenant's patience, a prospect's interest, or an owner's trust.

Midnight Texts

Tenants don't have emergencies during business hours. A leaky pipe at 2 AM means a voicemail you won't hear until morning — and water damage you'll pay for all week.

Lost Prospects

A renter finds your listing, sends a message, and hears nothing for 24 hours. By then they've signed a lease somewhere else. Every vacant day costs ~$100.

Maintenance Chaos

"Something is leaking." Which unit? Which fixture? You dispatch a plumber; it was the dishwasher. Wrong vendor, wasted trip, angry tenant.

Vendor Coordination

Schedule the plumber, notify the tenant, confirm access, follow up on completion. For one work order. Now multiply by 50 units.

"The biggest cost in property management isn't maintenance — it's the time spent coordinating maintenance."

The Solution: An AI Agent for Every Property

Your OpenClaw agent runs 24/7 in its own isolated environment. It answers tenant texts, triages maintenance with photos, responds to prospect inquiries in under 60 seconds, and sends you a summary — not every individual message. You manage properties. The agent manages communication.

Tenants, prospects, and vendors communicate with the agent. You get summaries and a dashboard.

How It Handles Your Day

Maintenance Triage & Dispatch

Tenant sends a photo and description. The agent uses vision to identify the issue, classifies the trade (plumber, electrician, HVAC, handyman), assigns a priority (1–10), and dispatches your preferred vendor. It follows up the next day to confirm resolution.

  1. Tenant texts issue + photo
  2. Agent identifies the problem and required trade
  3. Matches to your vendor list by trade, availability, rating
  4. Sends work order to vendor with unit access details
  5. Confirms appointment with tenant
  6. Follows up next day: "Was the issue resolved?"

Leasing & Showing Scheduling

When a prospect inquires about a listing, the agent responds in under 60 seconds with unit details, pricing, availability, and showing times. It handles the back-and-forth scheduling, sends reminders, and follows up after the showing with an application link.

  1. Prospect finds your listing, sends inquiry via text or listing platform
  2. Agent responds instantly with unit details and available showing times
  3. Prospect picks a time, agent confirms and sends directions
  4. Reminder text morning-of: address, parking, what to bring
  5. Post-showing follow-up: "How'd you like it? Here's the application link"

Rent Reminders & Late Follow-Up

Five days before rent is due: friendly reminder. Day-of: confirmation. Three days late: firm but professional follow-up. Seven days: escalation to you. The agent handles the awkward conversations so you don't have to, and tracks payment status across your entire portfolio.

  1. Day −5: "Friendly reminder, rent is due on the 1st"
  2. Day 1: "Rent is due today — here's the payment link"
  3. Day +3: "We haven't received your rent yet. Please submit by Friday to avoid late fees"
  4. Day +7: Escalates to you with tenant payment history
  5. Tracks who's paid across all units in the dashboard

Wiring the Agent

Setting up the infrastructure — Machine, channels, tools — takes about 15 minutes from the dashboard. But the infrastructure isn't the interesting part. The interesting part is what happens inside the agent: the prompt that defines its behavior, the coordinator that routes every conversation, the guardrails that keep it from going sideways, and the feedback loops that make it better over time.

Infrastructure Setup

From the dashboard, you create a Machine (isolated MicroVM), connect channels (SMS via Twilio, WhatsApp Business), enable tools (browser, image analysis, web search), and load your property data — units, tenants, vendors, listings, policies. This gives the agent something to work with. But it doesn't tell the agent how to work.

When a tenant texts at 11 PM, the prompt is what determines whether the agent dispatches a plumber, asks for a photo, or wakes up the property manager.

2. The Coordinator

A prompt tells the agent what to do. The coordinator determines when and how. Every incoming message passes through a classification and routing pipeline before the agent generates a response. This is what keeps 47 units' worth of concurrent conversations from turning into chaos.

1

Message Arrives

Tenant texts +1 (714) 555-0192. Channel adapter (Twilio webhook) receives the message with sender phone number, message body, and any media attachments (photos). Passed to the agent inside the MicroVM.

2

Sender Identification

Agent looks up the phone number against {{units}} data. Three outcomes:
Known tenant → load unit, lease dates, maintenance history from memory.
Known vendor → route to vendor coordination flow (work order updates).
Unknown number → classify as potential prospect or misdial.

3

Intent Classification

The agent classifies the message into a workflow: maintenance request, leasing inquiry, rent question, policy question, complaint, or general. If media is attached, image analysis runs first to inform classification. A photo of a leaking pipe + "help" = maintenance, priority 7, plumbing.

4

Context Assembly

Before generating a response, the coordinator assembles relevant context from persistent memory: the tenant's conversation history, any open maintenance tickets for their unit, upcoming lease renewal dates, and recent interactions. This is what lets the agent say "Is this the same dishwasher issue from two weeks ago?" instead of starting fresh every time.

5

Action Execution

The agent generates a response and any side-effect actions: dispatch a vendor (outbound SMS with work order), schedule a showing (calendar update + confirmation), log a maintenance ticket (persistent memory write), or escalate to the property manager (alert SMS). Each action passes through the guardrail layer before executing.

6

Follow-up Scheduling

After resolving the immediate request, the coordinator schedules follow-ups: check with tenant tomorrow if vendor was dispatched, follow up with prospect 2 hours after a showing, send rent reminder 5 days before due date. These scheduled actions re-enter the pipeline at the right time — the agent doesn't forget.

Concurrent by design. Marcus in 4B texts about his dishwasher at the same time a prospect asks about unit 7C and another tenant reports a noise complaint. The coordinator handles each as an independent conversation thread with its own context window. One machine, many concurrent conversations, zero cross-talk.

3. Guardrails & Validation

An autonomous agent that can dispatch vendors and text tenants needs hard limits. The guardrail layer sits between the agent's intent and every outbound action. Nothing leaves the system without passing through it.

Spending Limits

Every vendor dispatch has an estimated cost. Below the configured threshold ($500 default), the agent dispatches autonomously. Above it, the action is held and the property manager gets an approval request via SMS: "AirPro HVAC quotes $800 for compressor replacement at Unit 4B. Approve?" One reply: "yes" or "no."

Escalation Triggers

Certain keywords and intents always bypass the agent. Legal threats, lease changes, eviction mentions, safety concerns — these immediately notify the property manager with the full conversation transcript. The agent tells the tenant: "I'm connecting you with your property manager directly."

Message Review

Before every outbound message, a validation pass checks: Does the message contain information not in the agent's knowledge base? Does it make promises about timelines or costs? Does it reference the correct unit and tenant? Messages that fail validation are held for review instead of sent.

Action Audit Trail

Every action the agent takes — message sent, vendor dispatched, ticket created, escalation triggered — is logged with a timestamp, the conversation that triggered it, and the reasoning. The property manager can review the full trace: "Why did the agent dispatch a plumber at 2 AM?" Open the log, read the chain.

4. How the System Improves

Day one, the agent works from its prompt, your data, and its rules. Week two, it's better — not because the model changed, but because the system learns from what happens.

Property Manager Review

Every morning, the daily digest includes agent actions from the last 24 hours. The PM can flag any response as good, wrong, or needs adjustment. Wrong responses get corrected in the prompt rules. "When a tenant asks about pool hours, the answer is 6am-10pm, not 8am-8pm." The correction is immediate — update the policy data, the agent reflects it on the next conversation.

Escalation Pattern Analysis

The system tracks what the agent escalates and why. If the agent is escalating the same type of question repeatedly — "Can I install a ring doorbell?" "Can I paint my walls?" — it surfaces this as a gap: "Tenants are frequently asking about modifications. Consider adding a modifications policy to the knowledge base." You add a policy, the escalations stop.

Conversation Quality Metrics

The dashboard tracks: average response time, resolution rate (did the tenant's issue get resolved without escalation?), tenant sentiment (positive / neutral / negative across conversations), and vendor dispatch accuracy (was the right trade sent the first time?). These aren't vanity metrics — they tell you where the prompt needs tightening.

Knowledge Base Growth

Every conversation teaches the system something. A tenant asks "Is there EV charging?" — the agent doesn't know, escalates, the PM answers "No, but we're adding stations in Q3." That answer gets added to the knowledge base. Next time someone asks, the agent handles it directly: "We don't have EV charging currently, but stations are planned for Q3 2026." The knowledge base grows from real tenant questions, not guesswork about what tenants might ask.

The improvement loop in practice: Week 1 — the agent handles 60% of conversations autonomously, escalates 40%. Week 4 — you've plugged the gaps surfaced by escalation analysis, added policies for the most common questions, and tuned the maintenance triage thresholds. The agent now handles 85% autonomously. Same model, same prompt structure, better data and tighter rules.
